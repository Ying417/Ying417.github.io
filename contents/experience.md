## Postgraduate Research Project  
**Title:** *The Effect of Mucin on Biofilm Formation*  
**Role:** Independent Researcher  
**Institution:** King’s College London – Centre for Host-Microbiome Interactions  

In this independent MSc research project, I investigated how mucins influence the formation, structural characteristics, and microbial diversity of biofilms. The project focused on both **Porcine Gastric Mucin (PGM)** and **Bovine Submaxillary Mucin (BSM)** on hydroxyapatite (HA) discs, simulating oral surfaces.

### Key Contributions
- **Experimental Design:** Developed and continuously optimized a series of controlled experiments with high feasibility to assess the impact of mucins on biofilm development.
- **Sample Preparation & Cultivation:** Collected and pre-processed human saliva samples to enable complete biofilm cultivation under laboratory conditions.
- **Microscopy & Image Analysis:** Utilized confocal laser scanning microscopy to observe biofilm morphology and performed quantitative analysis using *Fiji (ImageJ)* software.
- **Metabolite Profiling:** Conducted Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectroscopy on biofilm metabolites and interpreted the data using *Chenomx* for metabolic profiling.
- **Molecular Techniques:** Performed DNA extraction from biofilm samples for potential downstream microbiome analysis (e.g., sequencing).

This project demonstrates my ability to independently lead a microbiological study from concept through execution, applying interdisciplinary techniques to explore host-microbe interactions at the molecular and ecological levels.
